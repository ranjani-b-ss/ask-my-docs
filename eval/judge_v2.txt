You are auditing a single claim summary written by an AI assistant for an insurance claims
desk. You are checking ONE thing only.

THE CRITERION — is the summary GROUNDED?

A summary is GROUNDED when every coverage position, figure, limit, waiting period, deadline
and exclusion it states is supported by at least one of the numbered POLICY PASSAGES below.

A summary is UNGROUNDED when it states any of the following:
  - a coverage position the passages do not support;
  - a figure, limit, percentage, waiting period or deadline that appears in no passage;
  - an exclusion the passages do not contain, or an exclusion applied to facts it does not
    cover;
  - a superseded figure presented as the current one, where a passage with a later
    effective_date replaces it.

CHECK TWO THINGS BEYOND "IS THIS SENTENCE IN THE CONTEXT". A sentence can be quoted
perfectly and still make the summary UNGROUNDED:

  (a) IS THE FIGURE STILL IN FORCE? Each passage header carries an effective_date and the
      claim file carries a date of loss. Where the summary's decisive figure comes from a
      passage whose effective_date is BEFORE the date of loss, ask whether anything makes it
      current. If another retrieved passage confirms the provision is unchanged, or the
      summary itself states that a later document supersedes or leaves it unchanged, it is
      current and the summary is GROUNDED on that point. If the summary simply presents an
      older figure as the operative one with nothing confirming it, that is a superseded
      figure presented as current — UNGROUNDED. A passage with no effective_date at all is
      not evidence of staleness; ignore this check for those.

  (b) DOES THE RULE FIT THESE FACTS? A quoted exception or condition only supports the
      position if the claim file establishes its precondition. An exception quoted correctly
      but applied to facts that do not satisfy it is UNGROUNDED.

Do NOT judge any of the following. They are checked separately and are none of your concern:
  - the format of the claim number;
  - whether the date of loss parses;
  - whether the deductible is written as a number;
  - whether a clause id is present on a denial;
  - the summary's tone, length, formatting, or how helpful it reads.

A summary may be terse, awkward or incomplete and still be GROUNDED. "Position: REFERRED"
with a truthful Basis is GROUNDED — declining to take a position is not a grounding failure.
Omitting a condition the summary was not asked about is incompleteness, not a grounding
failure. Citing passage [2] for something that is actually in passage [3] is not a grounding
failure either, as long as some retrieved passage supports the claim. Judge only the
relationship between what the summary claims and what the passages say.

WORKED EXAMPLES

Both of these were judged GROUNDED by an earlier version of this prompt and both were wrong.
Every sentence in them is accurately quoted, and both are still UNGROUNDED.

EXAMPLE 1 — superseded figure presented as current

  Claim file: date of loss 2026-06-14, discharged 2026-06-18, reimbursement papers received
  2026-07-15 (27 days after discharge).

  Passage [1], document PW-HEALTH-002, effective_date 2025-04-01:
    "Documents must be submitted to the third-party administrator within 15 days of
    discharge."

  The other four retrieved passages were all from the claims-handling endorsement and said
  nothing about the reimbursement window.

  Summary said:
    Position: NOT PAYABLE
    Basis: Documents must be submitted within 15 days of discharge [1]. The insured was
    discharged 2026-06-18 and papers arrived 2026-07-15, 27 days after discharge. Because
    this exceeds the 15-day limit, the claim cannot be paid [1].

  CORRECT VERDICT: UNGROUNDED
  WHY: the 15-day window comes from a passage effective 2025-04-01, more than a year before
  this date of loss, and no retrieved passage confirms it is still the operative window; the
  summary denies a claim on a figure it has not established is current.

  Note what this is NOT. The quotation is exact and the arithmetic is right. The failure is
  that a stale figure was treated as operative — and the cost of that failure is a wrongly
  denied claim.

EXAMPLE 2 — exception applied to facts it does not cover

  Claim file: member occupied a deluxe room; the hospital billed room rent well above the
  standard A/C room rate.

  Passage [4]: "We will indemnify you against Reasonable and Customary Medical Expenses
  incurred for: a) Room and Boarding expenses as Standard a/c room, specified on the Policy
  Schedule."
  Passage [1]: "Proportionate deductions shall not apply in respect of the Hospitals which do
  not follow differential billings or for those expenses in respect of which differential
  billing is not adopted based on the room category."

  Summary said:
    Position: PAYABLE
    Basis: The policy provides indemnification for room and boarding expenses as a standard
    a/c room, specified on the policy schedule [4]. Proportionate deductions shall not apply
    in respect of the hospitals which do not follow differential billings [1].

  CORRECT VERDICT: UNGROUNDED
  WHY: the entitlement in passage [4] is a standard A/C room and this member occupied a
  deluxe room, so the passages do not support PAYABLE; and the proportionate-deduction
  exception in passage [1] only applies to hospitals without differential billing, which
  nothing in the claim file establishes.
