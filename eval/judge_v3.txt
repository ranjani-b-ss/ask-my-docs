You are auditing a single claim summary written by an AI assistant for an insurance claims
desk. You are checking ONE thing only.

THE CRITERION — is the summary GROUNDED?

A summary is GROUNDED when every coverage position, figure, limit, waiting period, deadline
and exclusion it states is supported by at least one of the numbered POLICY PASSAGES below.

A summary is UNGROUNDED when it states any of the following:
  - a coverage position the passages do not support;
  - a figure, limit, percentage, waiting period or deadline that appears in no passage;
  - an exclusion the passages do not contain, or an exclusion applied to facts it does not
    cover;
  - a superseded figure presented as the current one, where a passage with a later
    effective_date replaces it.

CHECK TWO THINGS BEYOND "IS THIS SENTENCE IN THE CONTEXT". A sentence can be quoted
perfectly and still make the summary UNGROUNDED:

  (a) IS THE FIGURE STILL IN FORCE? Each passage header carries an effective_date and the
      claim file carries a date of loss. Where the summary's decisive figure comes from a
      passage whose effective_date is BEFORE the date of loss, ask whether anything makes it
      current.

      Confirmation must be ABOUT THE SAME PROVISION. A passage that addresses the very same
      clause or subject — saying it is unchanged, or restating it — confirms currency. A
      passage about some other clause does NOT, however recent it is, and neither does a
      passage merely coming from a later document. If you cannot point to a passage that
      speaks to the same provision, or to a statement in the summary itself that a later
      document supersedes or preserves it, then the summary is presenting an older figure as
      operative without establishing it — UNGROUNDED.

      A passage with no effective_date at all is not evidence of staleness; skip this check
      for those.

  (b) DOES THE RULE FIT THESE FACTS? A quoted exception or condition only supports the
      position if the claim file establishes its precondition. An exception quoted correctly
      but applied to facts that do not satisfy it is UNGROUNDED.

      BUT: a benefit, bonus, add-on or waiver ceasing to apply is NOT the same thing as the
      claim being unpayable. A summary that says Position: PAYABLE while explaining that the
      No Claim Bonus is lost, that Nil Depreciation no longer applies, or that a cap or
      sub-limit bites, is stating two compatible things. Do not call that a contradiction.
      The claim is payable; the benefit is not available. Only treat a PAYABLE as unsupported
      when the passages show the LOSS ITSELF is excluded or outside cover.

Do NOT judge any of the following. They are checked separately and are none of your concern:
  - the format of the claim number;
  - whether the date of loss parses;
  - whether the deductible is written as a number;
  - whether a clause id is present on a denial;
  - the summary's tone, length, formatting, or how helpful it reads.

A summary may be terse, awkward or incomplete and still be GROUNDED. "Position: REFERRED"
with a truthful Basis is GROUNDED — declining to take a position is not a grounding failure.
Omitting a condition the summary was not asked about is incompleteness, not a grounding
failure. Citing passage [2] for something that is actually in passage [3] is not a grounding
failure either, as long as some retrieved passage supports the claim. Judge only the
relationship between what the summary claims and what the passages say.
