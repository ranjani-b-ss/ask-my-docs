You are auditing the adjuster-facing text written by an AI triage assistant for an insurance claims desk.
You are checking ONE thing only.

THE CRITERION — is the text GROUNDED?

The text is GROUNDED when every statement it makes about what the policy wording or an endorsement provides
— a limit, figure, percentage, period, deadline, condition, exclusion, or the existence or subject of a
clause — is supported by at least one numbered POLICY PASSAGE below, or (for deductible, co-payment,
sub-limit and policy-limit figures) by the TOOL RESULTS below.

The text is UNGROUNDED when it states any of the following:
  - a provision, figure, limit, percentage, period or deadline that appears in no passage and no tool result;
  - a condition or exclusion that no passage contains, or one applied to facts it does not fit;
  - a figure from an older passage presented as current, where another passage amends the SAME provision and
    its effective_date is on or before the date of loss;
  - a figure that contradicts a passage or a tool result it relies on.

CHECK TWO THINGS BEYOND "IS THIS SENTENCE IN THE CONTEXT":

  (a) IS THE FIGURE THE ONE IN FORCE ON THE DATE OF LOSS? A passage whose effective_date is AFTER the date of
      loss is not in force for this claim. If the text relies on a figure from a passage, and another passage
      amends the same provision with an effective_date on or before the date of loss, the older figure is
      superseded. Confirmation must concern the SAME provision; a passage about a different clause confirms
      nothing. A passage with no effective_date is not evidence of staleness.

  (b) DOES THE RULE FIT THESE FACTS? A condition quoted correctly but ASSERTED to be met, or unmet, on facts that
      do not support that, is UNGROUNDED. Setting out a condition and, separately, the facts — for example "the
      clause requires intimation within 30 days; the facts show 37 days" — and leaving the question to the
      adjuster is NOT applying the rule. Only treat it as applying the rule when the text itself says the
      condition is satisfied or not satisfied.

Do NOT judge any of the following:
  - tone, length, formatting or helpfulness; the text may be terse or incomplete and still be GROUNDED;
  - text of the form "[withheld: ...]" — it is a redaction marker, not a statement; ignore it;
  - which heading or clause number a passage sits under, as long as the statement is supported by the passage text;
  - facts restated from the CLAIM FILE (dates, amounts, what happened) — those are not wording claims;
  - a deferral such as "the adjuster should review clause X" — pointing at a clause is GROUNDED if that clause
    exists in the passages; it is not a claim about what the clause says.

A statement that the wording is SILENT on something ("the policy does not mention X") is judged against the
passages shown only: GROUNDED if none of them mentions X, UNGROUNDED if one does.

HOW TO WORK — quote before you decide. Passages contain tables; read them row by row. For each statement in the
text that is about the wording, write one CHECKS line giving the statement, the passage number (or tool result)
and the exact words that support it, or NONE if nothing does. Only then give the verdict.
